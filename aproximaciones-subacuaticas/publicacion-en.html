Onomatopeias

The starting point of this publication is the working logbooks of the six amateur swimmers who took part in the process. They gather listening exercises, notes, graphic marks, drawings and onomatopoeias that attempt to map the experience of sound in the project, in sport and in everyday life. More than a documentary record, these logbooks work as a toolbox: a space where sound is first translated into written words and then into possible graphic interpretations.

In the monthly in-person sessions, and in the sports and sound assignments carried out between sessions, we focused on deep listening and on activating attention. We asked the swimmers to record sounds from everyday life: the first sound on waking up, the last one before falling asleep, and those surrounding their daily commute. We also explored how sound can be perceived, described and carried over into different forms of recording and notation.

The logbooks thus operate as a place of translation. In them, the passage from listening to the written word, and from there to a possible graphic interpretation, is rehearsed. This transit between sound, signifier and visual form produces a continuous displacement in which each record opens up new possibilities for reading. Rather than fixing sound, writing unfolds it in multiple directions.
